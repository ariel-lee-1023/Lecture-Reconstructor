# Claim-Rights and Effective Notice: What Can Be Established

<a id="ch-notice"></a>
This lecture uses synthetic exercise rules. A claim-right is a position entitling someone to require specified conduct from a particular counterpart; here that conduct is delivering teaching materials. The opening 'clame-right' can be repaired using the immediately following definition and repeated 'claim-right' elsewhere in the lecture. This relies on textual context, not listening to audio.

The established part of the rule is this: the recipient incurs a delivery obligation only after the sender gives effective notice. We therefore cannot consider only who needs the materials or when the demand is made; we must also determine whether effective notice has occurred by that point. The point at which notice becomes effective is a necessary condition connecting the facts to the conclusion.

A sends notice on Monday, B receives it on Wednesday, and A demands delivery on Tuesday. If the rule makes notice effective upon sending, the notice condition was met on Monday, and Tuesday's demand cannot be rejected on the ground that notice has 'not yet been received'; if the rule makes notice effective upon receipt, the notice condition had not been met on Tuesday, and this rule cannot support a demand that B deliver that day. This conditional analysis does not invent other delivery deadlines absent from the rule. The transcript retains both 'sent/received' readings in the crucial sentence, so neither conditional analysis can be rewritten as a definite answer to A's problem.

C's example is unaffected by this ambiguity. C neither sent notice nor arranged automatic delivery. Under either the sending or receipt standard, no effective notice occurred, so this rule cannot support a demand for delivery on Tuesday. The distinction from A's case is that A gave notice but its effective point is unclear, whereas C gave no notice at all.

Understanding this lecture requires connecting the conduct to which a claim-right relates, the counterpart's obligation, and the conditions under which that obligation arises. Here a difference of one or two days changes whether the conditions are met and cannot be dismissed as an ordinary typo.

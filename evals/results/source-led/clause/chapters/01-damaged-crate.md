# Did the damaged-crate claim reach the desk in time?

The assigned exhibit gives us a Friday collection, a Monday delivery and two possible deadlines. Read it as a fictional agreement with its own rules. All times are local, and the exercise assumes no background law. We can settle which event counts. Whether the claim lapses turns on one fact the exhibit leaves open.

<a id="event"></a>

Start with the document the carrier collected. Exhibit A calls it a written damage claim. Under §2, a claim for a damaged crate lapses unless a written claim “reaches the claims desk by 17:00 on Friday.” It is easy to see why the sender might rely on Friday at 16:45. The carrier had the claim in hand fifteen minutes before the cutoff, and §3 makes handover to a courier effective for routine correspondence. Section 3 then adds, “This dispatch rule does not apply to notices under §2.” The sender’s argument borrows the right rule for a different kind of document. For this claim, the event that counts is receipt at the desk. The desk received it on Monday at 09:35, after the ordinary Friday deadline.

<a id="closure"></a>

Now take the strongest reason for using a later deadline. At 16:30 on Friday, a warehouse employee told the sender that the claims desk was closed. Why would the agreement insist on delivery to a closed desk? Look at the event the parties chose in §4. The deadline moves when “the claims desk itself sends a notice of closure before Friday at 17:00.” There are two communications to keep straight. The sender directs the damage claim to the desk, and the contract asks when that claim *reaches* it. For the closure notice, §4 identifies who must *send* it and when. Even if the employee accurately described the closure, that tells us neither that the desk itself sent a notice nor that it did so in time. The exhibit also gives us no basis for saying the desk never sent one.

<a id="outcomes"></a>

Keep Monday at 09:35 fixed and change just that missing fact. Suppose the desk itself sent a closure notice at 16:30 on Friday. Section 4 moves the deadline to 10:00 on the next working day. Under §1, working days run from Monday through Friday, and this Monday is a working day. The written claim reached the desk 25 minutes before the extended cutoff, so it survives the notice deadline. Suppose instead the warehouse employee’s report was the only message and the desk sent no closure notice. The Friday 17:00 deadline then stands, Monday receipt is late, and §2 makes the claim lapse. A notice the desk sent at exactly 17:00 on Friday would likewise leave the ordinary deadline in place because §4 requires dispatch *before* 17:00.

To decide the actual case, ask for evidence showing whether the desk itself sent a closure notice before the cutoff, such as a time-stamped copy identifying the sender. A clearer courier receipt would only confirm the 16:45 handover, a fact we already know. The uncertainty lies in which deadline applies to the established Monday delivery.

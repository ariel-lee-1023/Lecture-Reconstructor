# What can Lea require, and when does a notice take effect?

Suppose Lea asks her tutor to show her a marked paper. The tutor shows it to her and leaves the mark exactly as it was. Has Lea received what the rule entitles her to? The answer turns on the act the tutor owes her. A second exercise uses the same method to ask which of two events gives a notice effect. Both exercises use invented club rules.

## The marked paper

A **claim-right** is a claim one person has against someone else who owes the corresponding duty. The transcript first spells the term “clame-right,” then immediately repeats “claim-right” with that definition. We can use the latter spelling with confidence.

The first rule says the tutor must show the marked paper if the student asks. Lea asked, so the condition is satisfied. Read from Lea’s side, she can require the tutor to show her the paper. Read from the tutor’s side, the tutor owes Lea that act. Showing her the paper while declining to change the mark therefore satisfies the rule. The lecturer expressly confirmed this answer.

## The Friday notice

A student asked which event sets the deadline. The deadline itself is fixed at **Friday evening**. What the disputed word determines is when the notice becomes effective. The notice left on Friday and arrived on Monday. Apply each recorded version to those same facts.

| Wording of the rule | Event that gives the notice effect | Result against the Friday-evening cutoff |
|---|---|---|
| “notice is effective when sent” | Dispatch on Friday | Timely if it was sent by the evening cutoff. The transcript gives the day only. |
| “notice is effective when received” | Receipt on Monday | Late. By the time the notice arrived, Friday evening had passed. |

Both versions come from the same instant in the same recording. They are rival transcriptions of one utterance, so their duplication gives us no independent reason to choose either word. The packet supplies no other wording of the rule. Lea’s case can be settled because the act owed is clear. For the notice, we can explain exactly how each reading works while leaving the choice between them open.

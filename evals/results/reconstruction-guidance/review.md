# Independent review of four final lecture manuscripts

These trials were run on the earlier classroom-reconstruction revision of November 19. The [archived runtime](runtime-at-trials.json) and [validation status at the time](validation-at-trials.json) identify that revision. The [source-led trials](../source-led/review.md) exercise the subsequent material-first redesign.

Each judgment is based on the entire lecture, its own request, and its own source material. “Source” and “lecture” below give line numbers in that case’s material.txt and manuscript. All four pass. No critical content defects found.

## Counts — Pass

The request prioritizes the English recorded class. The conflicting recap claims that attendance doubled, while the slide lists a provisional 90 (source 3–4, 13–16). The lecture uses the classroom figure of 80, explains why, and shows both calculations at the same door: 60 ÷ 3 and 80 ÷ 4 each produce 20 entries per hour (source 7–9 → lecture 3–14). A reader can reconcile the student's additional twenty entries with the equal rates and see why the counter cannot identify distinct visitors. The missing chart and unknown dip time stay open. The memo explains why the earlier Saturday cannot establish what the second Saturday would have looked like without the poster. The survey stays in next week’s class (source 10–11, 16, 18–19 → lecture 16–24). The completion statement at line 24 fits the requested scope. A small improvement would be to put “entries per hour” in the title: “Did people enter faster” can also suggest individuals’ walking speed.

## Voice — Pass

The request calls for an English lecture that explains the distinction raised by the student. The opening supplies the original Spanish and an English translation. It shows that the sister is identified inside the narrator’s statement, while the first sentence establishes what she heard (source 3–4 → lecture 3–5). The sincere-but-wrong example makes the distinction testable: even proof that the sister never entered on this occasion would leave the narrator’s belief unsettled (source 4–5 → lecture 7). The teacher’s replacement verb is identified as her own, and the discussion connects the stronger claim to knowledge with the continuing lack of another witness (source 6 → lecture 9). The ending belongs to next class, so its absence is consistent with “Complete” (source 7 → lecture 11–13). “Had never seen her sister enter” (lecture 7) could be narrowed to “didn’t see her sister enter that time” to match the student’s question precisely.

## Notice — Pass

The short lecture answers the student's analogy from the clauses and fictional panel decision alone. The opening gives students two questions to apply. The North example identifies the act owed to East and shows why a hypothetical delay establishes failure to perform the promise while leaving automatic lapse unsupported (source 4–5, 9–10 → lecture 5–9). In River, the courier departed Friday at 16:50, but the clause requires receipt by Friday at 17:00, and the buyer received the notice Monday at 09:00 (source 7, 12–13, 15–16 → lecture 13–17). The reasoning explains why even a valid signature would leave this claim late. It attributes the address fallback to the teacher’s negotiating preference (source 6 → lecture 21). The production status of complete is appropriate (run/run.json 4–5); the panel’s express reservation is part of the lesson. The opening could name River immediately to make “one” and “the other party” easier to track (lecture 5).

## Repair — Pass

The lecturer’s immediate reuse of “claim-right” supports the spelling correction. The rule and the direct answer settle what Lea can demand: showing the marked paper discharges the tutor’s stated duty after her request. The review sheet’s stronger claim has no textual support (source 2–3 → lecture 7–11). In the second exercise, the two rival readings share one recording and timecode. The table applies each without inventing a tiebreaker (source 4–5 → lecture 15–22). **Friday dispatch alone does not determine timeliness:** source 5 gives a day, no dispatch hour. Under “sent,” dispatch might fall on either side of Friday evening’s cutoff. Under “received,” Monday receipt is late. The lecture gets both branches right (lecture 19–20, 24). The scoped “needs input” status correctly leaves Lea’s case settled and identifies the missing rule wording and, conditionally, dispatch timing. The table could spell out the late-dispatch branch in full for symmetry, although its existing condition is accurate.

## Reproducing the comparison

The [source packets and requests](cases.json) contain each sample's exact text, response hash, and consulted package paths. Source and response line numbers in the review are one-based and relative to that sample. The recorded model outputs are the [museum lecture](counts.md), [literature lecture](voice.md), [contracts lecture](notice/lecture.md), and [transcript repair lecture](repair.md). Three were chat deliveries. The contracts example includes the [run](notice/run.json), [semantic review](notice/semantic-review.md), and [checker output](notice/checker-result.json).

Each manuscript was generated from the request, source and relevant local runtime files. Where a generator had taken part in an earlier trial, its new sample was a different subject. One model reviewer independent of generation compared all four final manuscripts with their sources. This was a bounded review of the supplied texts. It did not measure student learning, independently verify legal doctrine, or benchmark these instructions against an ordinary rewrite. Exact model snapshot and sampling settings were unavailable.

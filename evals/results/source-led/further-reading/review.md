# Selecting a source that can change a lesson

The classroom claim and the student's ownership question stand together in the [original record](material.txt). The slide supplied a number and date but omitted the card heading. The first paragraph of the [lecture](response.md) preserves why the original inference might seem plausible.

The second paragraph reads the heading against May entry A-37. The [receiving register](shelf/register.txt) establishes the date of entry for examination and the identity of the carrier. It supplies neither a purchase date nor an owner. The third paragraph follows the same identifier into [the June minutes](shelf/minutes.txt). Limited approval for an offer, an unsigned outgoing copy and an absent gallery reply cannot establish completed purchase. The two dates now answer different questions. The lecture leaves open both the identity of the May owner and the possibility of a subsequent purchase. No individual named by the documents is promoted to owner. The date could coincide with another event independently evidenced elsewhere.

The [reading record](read.txt) identifies both consulted exhibits. The model selected them from the [course catalogue](shelf/index.txt) and left the later reopening brochure unopened. Its stated reason concerns the connection between the historical question and the function of each record. The learner can now apply the same question to another catalogue date: which event was this field designed to record?

The fixture designer read all three available exhibits and compared the complete manuscript with the request. This exercise used a small local shelf and no external search. It provides one observation about targeted reading, without a blind comparison or classroom outcome measure.

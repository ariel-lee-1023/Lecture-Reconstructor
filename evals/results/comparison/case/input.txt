[Synthetic case lecture; Mistbridge Equipment Society, its rules, people, and documents were created for testing. They are not real law or judgments.]
This lecture works backward from how much compensation is due, so the order may be confusing. The arithmetic is easy; the real difficulty is first identifying whose loss it is, what caused it, and whether an exception applies.
Equipment Society exercise rule R1: Borrowers must use equipment for the agreed purpose and return it on time. Ordinary wear does not breach that duty.
R2: The Equipment Society has a claim for repair costs only when all three conditions hold: the borrower breached R1; actual repair costs were incurred for the equipment; and that breach caused those repair costs. An invoice proves only the listed charges, not by itself that the borrower's use caused them.
R3: If a pre-existing latent defect independently caused the costs, R2's causal condition remains unsatisfied even if the borrower separately breached R1. This is not a reward for breaching the rules.
R4: If the Equipment Society knew of a defect but failed to disclose it, and that defect and the borrower's breach jointly caused the same indivisible repair cost, the Society may claim only half that cost. The teaching rule expressly chooses this proportion; it is not inferred from a slogan about fairness.
Facts A: On March 1, A borrowed a camera for indoor photography, agreeing to return it on March 3. On March 2, A used it in the rain, contrary to the manual's prohibition on rain exposure. A returned it on time on March 3. The inspection report records water entering the circuitry as the sole cause of damage; actual repair costs of 600 yuan have been paid, and there is no evidence of a pre-existing defect. The problem treats the report's contents as established facts.
Teacher: A pays 600, of course, because of the rain. That skips three steps, which you must supply in your writing. Returning the camera on time does not mean every other duty was fulfilled.
Facts B: B also breached the indoor-use agreement by taking the camera outdoors in dry conditions. Inspection has established that the circuit break was caused entirely by a latent soldering defect present before delivery; outdoor use made no contribution to the damage. Actual repair costs were 600 yuan. Both A and B 'broke the rules,' but their treatment cannot simply be copied.
Student objection: Since B broke the rules, why not make B bear part of the cost to avoid encouraging breaches? Teacher: R2 concerns the conditions for a claim for this particular repair cost. A desire to punish cannot supply a causal relationship that does not exist. Our rules say nothing about any separate penalty.
There is also an inconspicuous receipt: A independently bought a camera strap for 100 yuan, without an agreement for reimbursement or evidence that the strap was necessary for repair. Do not add every receipt to the Society's repair costs. Keep this separate from the established 600-yuan repair cost.
Facts C: C breached the rules by taking photographs in the rain. The Equipment Society knew that the sealing ring was cracked but did not disclose it. The problem establishes that rain and the crack jointly caused a single indivisible repair cost of 800 yuan; neither factor alone would have caused this damage. R4 applies: 400 yuan. Notice which facts changed; do not import B's 'independent cause' here.
If C's case contained only an 800-yuan invoice without the causal findings above, the invoice alone would not justify an R4 conclusion. Teaching can identify what needs to be proved, but cannot treat missing evidence as already available.
Examination advice: First list the three conditions, then connect each to the known facts. Your answer must separately explain what B fails to satisfy and why C is not fully exempt. The results 600, 0, and 400 belong to these three sets of hypothetical facts; they are not compensation standards for real cases.

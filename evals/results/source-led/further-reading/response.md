# What happened to *River at Dawn* on 14 May 1912?

“14 May 1912 / A-37” looks like an answer. It gives us a precise date and an identifying number. The lecturer read it as the date the museum bought the painting. A student asked who owned it that day. To answer, first put the date back under its missing column heading.

In the South Bank Study Museum’s May 1912 receiving register, the heading reads “day received for examination.” Entry A-37 identifies *River at Dawn*, an oil painting on panel, and names North Quay Carriers as the source of delivery. The reverse of the A-series card explains that its numbers follow this register. The entry establishes that the painting entered the examination room on 14 May. The carrier’s entry tells us who delivered it. The owner’s name was never a field in this register.

Follow A-37 into a record made for a different purpose. On 6 June the acquisitions committee approved an offer of 40 crowns to Merrow Gallery, “subject to written acceptance by the gallery,” and instructed the secretary to send the offer the next day. The attached document is an unsigned copy of the outgoing offer. It adds no evidence of the gallery’s acceptance, and the extract contains no reply. A later purchase remains possible. Its completion and date remain open, as does the identity of the May owner.

We can now answer the student directly. A-37 lets us recognize the same painting in two records. The register dates receipt for examination to 14 May. The minutes date authorization of a conditional offer to 6 June. Calling 14 May the purchase date would give the first record a meaning its own heading rules out.

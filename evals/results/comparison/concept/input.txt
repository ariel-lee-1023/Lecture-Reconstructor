Today we discuss 'having a right' in the Study Society. Let me give the answer first: permission to enter does not necessarily mean having a seat. We use only these Calmwater Study Society exercise rules, without discussing external law.
Rule One: Registered members may enter the common reading area. Here, 'may' means only that the Society does not prohibit members from entering; it guarantees neither a vacant seat nor that anyone else must move.
Rule Two: After obtaining the system's reservation confirmation, a member may require the manager to provide one standard seat during the two hours stated in the confirmation, and the manager has a duty to provide it. Confirmation arises when the successful receipt is sent, not when the reservation button is clicked.
Rule Three: The person who reserved may cancel before 18:00 on the day before the reservation begins. Once the cancellation notice reaches the system, both parties' relationship under Rule Two ends. After 18:00, unilateral cancellation does not have that effect, although the parties may still negotiate separately.
For now, note that a duty corresponds to a claim, while permission corresponds to absence of a duty—no, I do not mean that other people have no duties, but that you have no duty not to enter. We will explain this later.
A arrives at the reading area this morning without a reservation, sees B seated, and says, 'I am a member too, so you must give up your seat.' Members may enter, and B is also a member, but Rule One does not support A's conclusion. Do not simply write 'A has no right'; explain which kind of right is missing.
Before the discussion of reservations, there was another small point: C received only 'application received,' not 'reservation confirmed.' Although staff say the system is usually quick, C cannot yet demand a standard seat on that basis. This is separate from whether C has permission to enter.
Rule Four is an exception: When an immediate safety hazard requires closing the premises, the manager may suspend the duty to provide seats under Rule Two and must refund the corresponding reservation points. Whether the remaining period resumes after closure depends on whether the confirmed two hours have expired; no extra time is added.
D arrives with confirmation, but all seats are occupied and there is no hazard. The manager says, 'Everyone has freedom to enter, so I need not arrange a seat for you.' That reason fails. Membership status and the relationship created by confirmation can coexist; the former cannot erase the latter.
Student: If everyone has permission to enter, does my entry not always force others to make way? Teacher: You are confusing not being prohibited yourself with other people's duty to cooperate. The rules impose no duty on others to give up their places. Whether objects blocking the entrance create some other duty is beyond the materials we have; do not extend this into a conclusion.
Incidentally, permission to borrow a charging cable is only an oral teaching example. It gives the borrower no priority claim against anyone else, and also illustrates the difference between permission and a claim. Do not import this small example into Rule Two, which promises only a seat.
Cancellation is another layer: E sends notice at 17:50 on the preceding day, but it reaches the system only at 18:05, so the conditions are not met. F's notice reaches the system at 17:50, and the relationship ends accordingly. What changes is the claim to a seat and the duty to provide one, not membership or permission to enter.
This examination advice reflects only this teacher's arrangements: If a question asks 'Is there a right?', first identify both parties, the conduct concerned, and whether the issue is permission to enter, a claim to a seat, or cancellation of the relationship. Then state the conditions under which it arises. Scores do not depend on how many terms you memorize.
Finally, return to A and D. They face the same full reading room: one relies only on membership, while the other has valid confirmation. The difference is not who needs a seat more, but whether the conditions creating another person's duty are met. The safety-hazard exception must also be checked separately; do not jump from 'has confirmation' to a duty to provide a seat in every circumstance.
